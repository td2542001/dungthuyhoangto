I am an M.S. researcher in Bio-Convergence Sciences at **Jeonbuk National University (JBNU)**, South Korea, working with Prof. Sadia Ameen. My work centers on **designing inorganic functional materials** and understanding how their structure controls their interaction with pollutants.

My background is in chemical engineering with a focus on inorganic materials. I started with **photocatalytic materials and self-cleaning coatings**, then moved to **high-entropy layered double hydroxides (HE-LDHs)**, layered materials in which five metal cations share one hydroxide lattice. My current project grows MgAlZnNiCo HE-LDH on nitrogen-doped biochar for detecting the persistent "forever chemical" PFHxA in water.

**In the lab, I work hands-on with:**
- Hydrothermal and co-precipitation synthesis of LDHs and carbon composites
- Structural characterization (XRD, FTIR, FE-SEM)
- Electrochemical testing (CV, EIS) and electrode fabrication
- Process simulation (Aspen Plus)

Looking ahead, I am interested in **combining experiments with AI and data-driven methods**. High-entropy materials have an enormous composition space that cannot be explored by trial and error alone, and I want to use machine learning together with high-throughput synthesis and characterization to identify promising compositions faster.

I genuinely enjoy learning. Moving from chemical engineering to photocatalysis, electrochemistry, and now high-entropy materials has taught me how to pick up new techniques quickly, and I am excited to keep learning new tools, from advanced characterization to programming and machine learning.

I am looking for **PhD opportunities starting in Fall 2027** in inorganic materials chemistry, especially layered and high-entropy materials, including **AI-assisted materials discovery** for environmental and energy applications.
