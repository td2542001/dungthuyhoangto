Research question
------
Different interlayer anions may bind to the positively charged HELDH layers with different strength. This could change how easily PFHxA<sup>&minus;</sup> exchanges into the structure and, in turn, the electrochemical sensing performance. This project asks which interlayer anion best suits PFHxA sensing.

Material design
------
- **MgAlFeNiCo HELDH:** five cations share one brucite-like hydroxide lattice. Mg supports the structure, Al<sup>3+</sup> and Fe<sup>3+</sup> give the layers their positive charge, and Ni and Co provide redox-active sites.
- **TPA-TP-COF:** a crystalline covalent organic framework with triazine and pyridine units, used as a hierarchical porous scaffold to improve HELDH dispersion, surface area and mass transport.
- **Composite (HELDH@COF):** the HELDH and COF are synthesized separately and then combined.

Interlayer anions compared
------
The usual LDH affinity order is NO<sub>3</sub><sup>&minus;</sup> &lt; Cl<sup>&minus;</sup> &lt; SO<sub>4</sub><sup>2&minus;</sup> &lt; CO<sub>3</sub><sup>2&minus;</sup> (Miyata, 1983). A weakly held anion should be replaced more easily.

| Interlayer anion | Affinity for the layers | Expected exchange with PFHxA<sup>&minus;</sup> |
|---|---|---|
| NO<sub>3</sub><sup>&minus;</sup> | Lowest | Highest |
| Cl<sup>&minus;</sup> | Low to moderate | Partial |
| SO<sub>4</sub><sup>2&minus;</sup> | High | Limited |
| CO<sub>3</sub><sup>2&minus;</sup> | Highest | Little or none; binding mainly at outer surfaces and edges |

Approach
------
- Synthesize HELDH with each interlayer anion and check the structure by XRD and FTIR
- Prepare the HELDH@COF composites and modified glassy carbon electrodes
- Compare the electrochemical response to PFHxA by CV and EIS

<details>
<summary><b>Mechanism: how could PFHxA enter the layers?</b></summary>

<ol>
<li><b>Electrostatic attraction.</b> The hydroxide layers carry a permanent positive charge, and PFHxA is present in water as the carboxylate anion C<sub>5</sub>F<sub>11</sub>COO<sup>&minus;</sup>.</li>
<li><b>Anion exchange.</b> PFHxA<sup>&minus;</sup> replaces the interlayer anion while the layer charge stays balanced: one PFHxA<sup>&minus;</sup> for each NO<sub>3</sub><sup>&minus;</sup> or Cl<sup>&minus;</sup>, and two for each CO<sub>3</sub><sup>2&minus;</sup> or SO<sub>4</sub><sup>2&minus;</sup>.</li>
<li><b>Interlayer accessibility.</b> A weakly held anion leaves more easily, so the gallery can open around the longer PFHxA<sup>&minus;</sup>. The COF pores may help carry PFHxA to the flake edges.</li>
<li><b>Signal.</b> PFHxA<sup>&minus;</sup> at and between the layers alters interfacial charge, which can be read out by CV and EIS.</li>
</ol>

<p><i>This mechanism is a working hypothesis and is still being tested.</i></p>
</details>
