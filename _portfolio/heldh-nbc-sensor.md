Why this matters
------
Per- and polyfluoroalkyl substances (PFAS) are persistent "forever chemicals" found in drinking water. Short-chain PFAS such as **perfluorohexanoic acid (PFHxA)** are increasingly used as replacements for long-chain compounds, but they are mobile in water and hard to monitor outside a specialized lab. Electrochemical sensors offer a low-cost, portable alternative to LC-MS/MS.

Material design
------
- **High-entropy LDH (HELDH):** five cations (Mg, Al, Zn, Ni, Co) share one brucite-like hydroxide lattice. Al<sup>3+</sup> makes the layers positively charged, while Ni and Co provide redox-active sites.
- **N-doped biochar (NBC):** biochar doped with nitrogen using urea and pyrolyzed under N<sub>2</sub>, giving a conductive carbon support.
- **Composite (HELDH@NBC):** HELDH nanoflakes grown on NBC sheets by in-situ co-precipitation, combining the anion affinity of the LDH with the conductivity of the carbon.

Synthesis and characterization
------
- Several HELDH compositions synthesized hydrothermally and compared
- Characterized by XRD, FTIR, FE-SEM and electrochemical methods
- The optimal composition forms thin, interconnected nanoflakes with an open porous network and high electrochemically active surface area

Electrode fabrication
------
- Glassy carbon electrodes polished sequentially with alumina slurry and checked by cyclic voltammetry in ferri/ferrocyanide
- Catalyst ink drop-cast in small successive layers to give a uniform film and avoid coffee-ring effects, with a minimal Nafion binder

Electrochemical testing
------
- **Cyclic voltammetry (CV)** in a ferri/ferrocyanide redox probe to compare charge transfer of bare, HELDH and HELDH@NBC electrodes
- **Electrochemical impedance spectroscopy (EIS)** to follow changes in charge-transfer resistance (R<sub>ct</sub>) after exposure to PFHxA
- Calibration across a range of PFHxA concentrations with blank buffer controls

<details>
<summary><b>Mechanism: why does HELDH@NBC capture PFHxA?</b></summary>

<ol>
<li><b>Electrostatic attraction.</b> The hydroxide layers carry a permanent positive charge, and PFHxA is present in water as the carboxylate anion C<sub>5</sub>F<sub>11</sub>COO<sup>&minus;</sup>.</li>
<li><b>Anion exchange and surface binding.</b> The carboxylate head can exchange with loosely held anions at layer surfaces and edges and form hydrogen bonds with surface M&ndash;OH groups.</li>
<li><b>Hydrophobic / fluorophilic interaction.</b> The perfluorinated tail favors non-polar surfaces such as the carbon network of the biochar.</li>
<li><b>Role of N-doped biochar.</b> Nitrogen sites add basic sites and improve conductivity, so binding events at the surface are transmitted to the electrode.</li>
<li><b>Signal.</b> PFHxA at the interface changes interfacial charge transfer, which is measured as a change in R<sub>ct</sub> by EIS.</li>
</ol>

<p><i>Whether the signal comes mainly from surface blocking or from anion exchange/intercalation is still being investigated.</i></p>
</details>
