MgAlFeNiCo-HELDH@TPA-TP-COF

Brucite-like [M(OH)6] octahedral layers sharing five cations, assembled on a porous TPA-TP-COF scaffold. Interlayer anion: .
How could PFHxA enter the layers? (proposed)
  1. Electrostatic attraction — Al3+ and Fe3+ make the hydroxide layers positive (+). PFHxA is a strong acid, so in water it is the anion C5F11COO−.
  2. Anion exchange — PFHxA− replaces the interlayer anion while the layer charge stays balanced (1 NO3− or Cl− ↔ 1 PFHxA−; 1 CO32− or SO42− ↔ 2 PFHxA−).
  3. Interlayer accessibility — a weakly held anion leaves more easily, so the gallery can open around the longer PFHxA−. The COF pores help carry PFHxA to the flake edges.
  4. Sensing signal — PFHxA− at and between the layers alters interfacial charge, read out by CV and EIS. Which anion gives the best response is the question being tested.
Layer affinity:
NO3− < Cl− < SO42− < CO32−
